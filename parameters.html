Choosing Parameters

Before a ceremony you choose three things: the quorum (k of n), the seed size, and the seed type. This page helps you pick values that match your custody policy.

The quorum: k of n

  • ``n`` is the total number of cards (and shares).

  • ``k`` is how many of them are required to reconstruct the secret.

The intuition:

  • A larger ``n`` gives more redundancy — you can afford to lose more cards (you can lose up to n - k and still recover).

  • A larger ``k`` gives more security — an attacker must compromise more cards — at the cost of needing more people/locations to recover.

Note

The two are independent. 3-of-5 survives the loss of 2 cards and requires 3 to recover; 2-of-5 survives 3 losses but only needs 2 to recover (weaker). Pick k from your security policy, then n from how much redundancy you want on top.

Constraints: n ∈ [2, 12], k ∈ [2, 7], and k ≤ n.

Note

n is also capped by the number of storage cards you hold: a set’s card count is the maximum n. You may use fewer (leaving spares unused), but never more. The k-of-n is your choice at the ceremony — it is not marked at the factory. See Card order (share numbers).

Warning

k = 1 is not supported (and would be insecure — a single card would reveal the secret). The minimum threshold is 2.

Note

For a Bitcoin treasury key, the common choice is -s 32 (256-bit). For an HSM AES wrapping key, choose the size of that key (16 or 32).

Seed type

The seed type (-t) tells the orchestrator how to generate or interpret the secret:

-t f

Fully random bytes with no structure. The most flexible choice — use it for AES keys, HMAC keys, or any opaque secret. Valid sizes: 16, 32, 48, 64, 128.

-t r

A NIST EC “r1” private key (a valid scalar for P-256 / P-384 / P-521). Use when the secret must be a valid EC private key on those curves. Valid sizes: 32, 48, 66.

-t k

A secp256k1 private key — the Bitcoin / Ethereum curve. Valid size: 32.

Tip

If you are protecting a Bitcoin key, use -t k -s 32. If you just need a random secret (e.g. an HSM wrapping key), use -t f.
